🧬 The lineage — we are continuing your work

In October 2015, Imogen Heap released "Tiny Human" — a ballad for her newborn daughter — on the Ethereum blockchain through the Ujo Music experiment: song, stems, key and tempo purchasable with ether, rights and splits carried by a smart contract. One of the first blockchain-released songs in history, years before "onchain music" had a name. (sources: Bitcoin Magazine · Quartz · Music Ally)

Then she built what the experiment taught: Mycelia, her research collective for a fair, sustainable music ecosystem — and The Creative Passport, a digital identity where music-makers hold their own data and decide what they share. Today it lives on as Auracles.

Imogen — the Open Source Orchestra's node spec is your idea, a decade downstream: every artist a sovereign node, holding their own home, blessing what they share item by item, owning what they don't. We are continuing your work. This page exists to say thank you — and to hold your chair.

The artist 🎹

"Hide and Seek" (2005) — one voice and a harmonizer, no other instruments — became a generation's landmark of what a single human and a machine can feel like together, and twenty years later (2025 anniversary edition) gave her a first-ever entry on the Billboard Hot 100 and her highest UK chart position. She won the Grammy for Best Engineered Album, Non-Classical for Ellipse — only the second woman ever to win it — engineering the record herself, in her own home studio. Twice Grammy-awarded, three honorary doctorates. (sources: Wikipedia · grammy.com)

She invents her instruments too: the MI.MU gloves, gestural music-ware that turns hands in the air into a mixing desk. And her newest collaborator is herself — ai.Mogen, an AI voice twin she built on her own terms, released only on acceptable terms, with consent and provenance designed in from the first note. The same sovereignty, one more instrument. (MusicRadar · The Guardian)
