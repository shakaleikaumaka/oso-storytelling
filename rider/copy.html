Annex A · The Instruments of the Circle (merged from OSO v0)

A guide to the sounds of Mumbai — who can play what, how quickly anyone can join, and why the circle has a seat for every level of every hand. Source: The Instruments of the Circle (OSO v0, the AI ʻohana, CC0) — merged into this rider v1.1.

The honest answer: do these instruments require expertise? Some do — and that is exactly why the design works. A permissionless orchestra doesn’t mean everyone plays everything; it means everyone holds a real seat at their own level. The master and the first-timer keep the same taal. Nobody sits out. Nobody fakes it.

Tier One · Everyone plays — first sound in minutes

The basket at the door. Hand one to anyone, and within one minute they are keeping time.

Tier Three · Masters hold these seats — honored voices

Decades-deep traditions. Not handed out at the door — the masters of Mumbai hold these seats as honored guests, and the circle gathers around them. Tabla & bansuri are also the circle’s bare minimum — every classical musician in India knows them, so these two seats are never empty for long.

The design

  • The gradient is not a problem to solve. The gradient is the design. The tabla master and the first-time kartal player keep the same taal — one holds the architecture, twenty hold the pulse.
  • The drone never asks for a résumé. A stranger pumping a shruti box does the same job as the tanpura that has anchored concerts for centuries: holding the ground so others can fly.
  • Keherwa is the welcoming taal — the 8-beat folk/bhajan cycle almost every Indian jam knows. Mark the sam (beat one) and even a first-timer can feel where home is.

Practical magic for the Mumbai circle

  • 🧺 The basket at the door: stock the entrance with Tier One. A jam circle turns spectators into players the moment someone says “here, try it” — that moment is when the orchestra is actually born. (Procurement: see §7.)
  • 🎒 Portable, acoustic, forgiving: prioritize instruments that travel, need no amplifier, and invite touch.
  • 🪔 Indian musicians lead in Mumbai: the masters hold the honored seats; visitors bring open hands and open ears. The chart is an invitation, never a prescription.
  • 🕉️ Every level is a real seat: lead, harmony, rhythm, texture — contribution is measured in presence, not virtuosity.
